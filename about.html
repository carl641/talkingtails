Dogs don't come with a manual.

Talking Tails exists to close the gap between what you're asking your dog for and what your dog actually understands. That's the whole job — everything else is technique.

Our story

Built around families, not competitions

We started Talking Tails because too many good owners were being handed a one-size-fits-all program and left to figure out the rest alone.

A training plan that ignores your work schedule, your kids, your fenceless yard or your dog's history isn't a plan — it's a worksheet. So we do it the other way around: watch the dog, listen to the household, then build the program.

The result is training that survives contact with real life. Not a dog who performs beautifully in a training room and falls apart at the front door, but one who is genuinely easier to live with on a Tuesday afternoon when nobody's watching.

Teddy Walls · Owner & head trainer

Meet the trainer

Teddy Walls

A lifelong dog handler with a background in criminal justice and professional canine instruction.

Teddy trained under several experienced professionals and completed a school dedicated to canine instruction before opening his own training business. His work has spanned the full range of the field — protection dogs at one end, family pets at the other.

That background shows up in the details: reading stress before it turns into a bite, knowing when to push a dog and when to back off, and being straight with owners about what a dog is and isn't likely to become.

He also does the unglamorous part well — explaining the same thing three different ways until it clicks for the person holding the leash.

How we train

Four things we hold to

01

Clarity before correction

A dog can't be held to a standard they were never taught. We teach the behavior thoroughly, in stages, before we ever expect it under pressure.

02

The relationship is the tool

Trust is what makes a dog choose you over a squirrel. We build engagement and confidence first — obedience follows a dog who actually wants to work with you.

03

Train the whole household

If one person's rules differ from everyone else's, the dog learns the loophole. Family members are welcome — and encouraged — at sessions.

04

Honest expectations

Some things take six weeks and some take six months. We'd rather tell you that at the free evaluation than sell you a timeline that was never real.

Who we work with

Every breed, every age, every starting point

Eight-week-old puppies and eight-year-old rescues. Ninety-pound shepherds and six-pound terriers. Dogs who are simply untrained, and dogs who have been through a program somewhere else that didn't hold.

We don't turn dogs away for being difficult. Reactivity, aggression, deep-set anxiety and bite history are all things we've handled — with a slower plan, tighter safety management and honest conversation about outcomes.

What we do ask is that you show up. Training is a shared project. The owners who get remarkable results are the ones who practice for ten minutes a day between sessions.
