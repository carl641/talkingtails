Why Talking Tails

Training the dog is half of it. The other half is you.

Most frustrating dog behavior isn't stubbornness — it's a breakdown in communication. Your dog doesn't know what you're asking, or they've learned that the answer is optional.

We fix both sides of that conversation. Your dog learns what each cue means and that it works the same way in the living room, the front yard and the middle of a busy sidewalk. You learn the timing, the body language and the follow-through that make it stick long after the last session ends.

Every program starts with a free evaluation, because no two dogs — and no two households — need the same plan. We'll meet your dog, listen to what's actually driving you crazy, and lay out honestly what training will take.

How it works

From first call to off-leash

No mystery, no pressure, no contract before you've met us. Here's exactly what working with Talking Tails looks like.

Free evaluation

We meet you and your dog, watch how they actually behave, and talk through what you want life to look like. You'll leave knowing what's realistic, how long it should take and what it costs — with no obligation.

A plan built for your dog

Breed, age, history, temperament and your household's routine all shape the program. An anxious rescue and a bulldozing adolescent lab do not get the same homework.

Training sessions

Your dog learns the skills; you learn how to run them. Every session ends with clear homework, so progress continues between visits instead of resetting.

Proofing in the real world

Commands are taken out of the quiet room and tested where it counts — the driveway, the park, the front door with a guest on the other side of it, other dogs walking past.

Support that doesn't expire

New house, new baby, new behavior at eighteen months? Call us. Ongoing guidance is part of the relationship, not an add-on.

Who you'll be working with

Trainers who've handled far more than the family pet

Talking Tails is led by Teddy Walls, a lifelong dog handler with a background in criminal justice and formal canine instruction.

He learned under several experienced professionals and completed a school dedicated to canine instruction before opening his own training business — and he's worked with everything from protection dogs to the family beagle who won't stop counter-surfing.

That range matters. A trainer who has handled serious working dogs reads pressure, drive and stress in a pet dog long before it becomes a problem, and knows the difference between a dog who won't and a dog who genuinely can't.
